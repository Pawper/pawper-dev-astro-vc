The strongest argument for writing decisions down before they're made is not that it produces better decisions — it's that it surfaces assumptions. When you write "we're doing X because Y," you have to know what Y is. Often you don't, and the act of writing reveals that.

This is different from writing a decision after the fact, which tends to rationalize rather than examine. Post-hoc documentation is useful for communication but not for thinking.

I try to write a one-paragraph decision record before any significant technical choice: what are we deciding, what are the options, and why are we picking this one. The record is usually wrong in some details. That's fine. The point is the discipline of making the reasoning explicit before the decision is locked in.
