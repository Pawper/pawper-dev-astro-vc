Removing a feature is harder than adding one, and not just technically. Adding a feature is a gift. Removing one feels like taking something away, even if no one used it, even if it was actively causing problems.

I've shipped removals that I was certain were right and still felt uncomfortable publishing the changelog entry. There's a fear that someone, somewhere, depended on the thing, and you're about to find out the hard way.

The data usually helps. Usage analytics make it easier to remove features when you can show that the removal affects a small or zero number of people. When you can't show that, the answer is to add instrumentation before you remove, wait a cycle, and then decide with evidence rather than intuition.
