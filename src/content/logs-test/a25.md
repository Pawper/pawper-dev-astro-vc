Every shipping cycle ends in one of two ways: you ship the thing, or you stop working on it. The second ending is more common than engineers usually acknowledge. Projects get deprioritized. Timelines slip past the point where the feature still makes sense. Something more urgent arrives. The thing doesn't ship.

What separates a useful incomplete project from a wasted one is what you extract from it. The code might be reusable. The research is almost always reusable. The decisions you made and documented are reusable. The mistakes are the most reusable of all, if you wrote them down.

Shipping in public is partly about the stuff you ship. It's also about building a practice where the work is legible enough that it retains value even when the cycle ends early.
