There's a hidden cost to building privately that doesn't show up until you're ready to ship: the cost of making it legible. Every assumption you made in isolation needs to be explained. Every convention you invented needs to be documented. The interface that made sense in your head needs to make sense to someone who wasn't in your head.

This isn't a reason to build everything in the open. Some work genuinely benefits from focus and privacy. But it's worth knowing that private work accumulates a translation debt that gets paid at shipping time.

The projects where I've felt most prepared to ship were the ones where I'd been narrating the work as I went — not necessarily publicly, but in a way that forced me to make the thinking legible before the code was done.
